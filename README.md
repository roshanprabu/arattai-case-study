# Arattai Product Case Study

A self-initiated PM case study on **Arattai**, Zoho's messaging app — built as a portfolio piece to demonstrate product thinking: framing ambiguous problems, forming and testing competing hypotheses, reasoning about network effects, and making explicit strategic trade-offs.

## What this is (and isn't)

This is **not** an internal Zoho/Arattai project. I never worked at Arattai, never had access to internal metrics, and never interviewed Arattai employees or users directly. Every claim in this repo is one of five things, and I've tried to label which:

1. **Verified fact** — confirmed by a primary source (Zoho/Arattai official channels, app store listings, direct leadership statements) or corroborated by multiple reputable secondary sources.
2. **Publicly available data** — a number or statement reported by a named source, with the date and exact definition recorded (e.g. "cumulative downloads" vs. "MAU" vs. "App Store rank" are not the same thing and are never conflated here).
3. **Reasonable inference** — a conclusion drawn from patterns in public data (e.g., review themes, timing correlations) that isn't itself directly stated anywhere.
4. **Assumption** — something I couldn't verify and am explicitly assuming for the sake of moving the analysis forward.
5. **Proposed product decision** — my own PM judgment call (strategy, MVP scope, metrics, experiments) — these are recommendations, not facts about what Arattai actually did or will do.

No fabricated user interviews, no fabricated quotes, no invented metrics, and no pretending any proposed solution was actually built or shipped.

## Methodology

Research prioritized primary sources (Zoho/Arattai official channels, app store listings, leadership interviews, government statements) and reputable secondary sources (major Indian business press, established tech journalism). SEO content farms, unsourced blogs, and AI-generated summaries were excluded as sources. Every quantitative claim is recorded with its number, date, source, and exact definition to avoid conflating downloads, installs, registrations, MAU, and DAU — a common error in coverage of this story.
